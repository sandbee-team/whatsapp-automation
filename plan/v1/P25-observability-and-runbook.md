# P25 — observability-and-runbook

**Goal (one line):** every metric the blueprint and the scope delta name is registered under the four-gauge label allow-list, scraped, alerted on and dashboarded from `infra/observability/`, an operator can act on any alert from `docs/RUNBOOK.md`, and CI proves no phone number, message body, email or API key reaches a log line, a metric label or an audit metadata value.
**Status:** done · **Size:** M · **Session:** 1 of 1
**Depends on:** P24 (must be `done`; P24 is the last phase that registers new metrics)
**Blocks:** P26, P27, P28

**Size warning:** this phase sits on the 10-step ceiling. If step 9 (the PII test + the alert demo — the
two things the gate is written against) is not green with time left, stop after step 9 and split:
step 10 (Grafana dashboards + the dashboard lint) becomes `plan/v1/P25a-dashboards.md`, added as a row in
`plan/README.md`. P26 depends on **P25**, not on P25a — dashboards are how a human sees the fleet, they are
not what the scale proof reads (it reads the scrape and Postgres rollups directly).

## What you are building (3-6 bullets)
- **One canonical metric inventory** in `@wp/domain` (name, type, labels, owning module, alerting yes/no) plus a CI guard that fails the build when a registered metric is missing from it, when an inventory entry has no registration, or when an `instance_id`/`client_id` label appears outside the four allow-listed gauges.
- The **missing metrics filled in** — every row of the blueprint's Observability table plus the scope-delta list (`wp_wallet_*`, `wp_broadcast_*`, `wp_inbound_shed_total`, `wp_receipts_total{event_type}`, `wp_receipt_unmatched_total`, `wp_inbound_dead_letters_total{error_class}`, `wp_group_sends_total`, `wp_signal_decrypt_failure_total{cause}`, `wp_stranded_epoch_jobs`, `wp_shed_no_taker_total`) and `wp_messages_out_without_job` (invariant 1 as a gauge).
- **Scrape + rollup**: an internal-only `/metrics` listener per role, and one ≥5-minute Postgres rollup collector for the fleet-level gauges that cannot come from a worker's memory.
- **`infra/observability/`**: Prometheus, Alertmanager, Loki, Grafana Alloy (log shipper with a low-cardinality label allow-list), Grafana provisioning, alert + SLO recording rules, and promtool rule unit tests.
- **`docs/RUNBOOK.md`**: one anchored section per alert, plus pause/resume, reconnect/QR, unresolved sends, Redis loss per instance, fence regression, key rotation, wallet drift, restore drill and deploy/drain.
- **The PII gate**: an end-to-end log-grep test over logs, the metric scrape and `audit_logs.metadata`, including the Postgres-error leak path.

## Risks / gotchas specific to this phase
- **The blueprint's per-client opt-out alert cannot be a Prometheus alert.** "Opt-out rate > 10/1,000 for a client" needs a `client_id` label, which the four-gauge rule forbids (scope delta *Observability*). Do not smuggle it in as a `{client}` series and do not silently drop the alert: implement it as a scheduled SQL check that writes a finding and calls `notify()`, and write that resolution into `docs/RUNBOOK.md`. The same applies to per-client wallet balance and per-client broadcast funnels — Postgres rollups, always.
- **Postgres errors are the classic PII leak.** `node-postgres` errors carry `detail` (often the offending row values), `where` and `internalQuery`. Pino's default `err` serializer prints all of them. The P01 field allow-list guards *log fields*, not the error object — the log-grep test will find this, and step 9's serializer fix is the reason it is a named step rather than a surprise.
- **Alert annotations are copy too.** `summary`/`description` render label values. Never template a phone number, JID, group subject, message body or wallet `external_ref` into an annotation, and never write a banned claim ("ban-proof", "guaranteed delivery") anywhere in `docs/RUNBOOK.md` — it goes through `check-copy.ts` for exactly that reason.
- **Loki labels are the second cardinality trap.** `client_id`/`instance_id` as Loki *stream labels* kills Loki the way they kill Prometheus. They stay JSON fields inside the line; the Alloy config's label set is `env,role,level` and the config is reviewed for that.
- **The rollup collector must not become an O(active instances) loop.** One aggregate statement at ≥ 300 s (ADR 0018 §4). If a panel needs a per-instance number sooner, it queries Postgres through the tenant API — that is what the four allow-listed gauges plus rollups are for.
- **A failing observability stack must never affect sending.** The collector, the metrics listener and Alloy are best-effort: a scrape failure, a Loki outage or a full disk logs and backs off. Nothing there may pause an instance, fail a job or block a claim (invariants 2 and 5 — fail-safe means *sending* stops on unclear provider state, not that monitoring gets to stop sending).
- **Promtool proves the rule, not the metric.** A promtool test passes happily against a metric name nothing emits. That is precisely why `check-metric-inventory` + the manifest cross-check exist; keep both, and do not "simplify" by deleting one.
- **Do not add `wp_instance_connected`.** Design §3.8 names it, P01 fixed the allow-list to `wp_instance_health_state`, `wp_instance_link_state`, `wp_instance_queue_depth`, `wp_instance_oldest_queued_seconds`. The session-availability SLO is a recording rule over `wp_instance_link_state`. Changing the allow-list here would silently break the P01, P09 and P16 tests.
- **Quote nothing.** Dashboard titles, runbook text and alert descriptions may not state a capacity or price figure. The 18/35 MB brackets may appear in the runbook only if labelled *derived, unmeasured until Gate B (P26)* — ADR 0018 §8.
- **Secrets stay out of the stack config.** Alertmanager receiver URLs and Grafana admin passwords come from `/etc/wp/secrets/*` file mounts (0400), never inline in a YAML that lands in the tree.
